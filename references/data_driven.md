# Data-Driven Discovery (H8-H12)

Collect new data or reanalyze existing data to spark ideas. This category focuses on how interaction with data—whether through collection, analysis, experimentation, or simulation—can generate new insights and hypotheses.

**Usage Note**: Be selective—choose the most relevant heuristic and develop ideas thoroughly. Quality and depth are more important than using multiple heuristics or generating many ideas.

## H8: Open-Ended Qualitative Exploration

**Core Principle:** Gather narratives or observations for thematic insight with immersive methods like case studies.

**Application:** Begin with exploratory qualitative research without rigid hypotheses. The depth and context provided by qualitative data often reveals phenomena, processes, and relationships that quantitative studies miss.

**Key Questions:**
- What patterns emerge from close observation?
- What do participants say that challenges existing theories?
- What contextual factors shape the phenomenon?
- What processes or mechanisms become visible through deep engagement?
- What concepts do participants use that researchers don't?

**Examples:**
- **Anthropology:** Conducting ethnographic fieldwork in communities to discover social practices that challenge Western assumptions
- **Business Research:** Observing workplace interactions to identify informal communication patterns affecting productivity
- **Medical Research:** Using patient interviews to identify side effects or quality-of-life impacts not captured in clinical measures
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Observing users in natural contexts to discover usability issues and unexpected usage patterns
- **Education:** Classroom observations revealing teaching strategies that correlate with student engagement

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Enter field with open mind and broad questions
2. Use methods appropriate to context:
   - Ethnography (participant observation)
   - In-depth interviews
   - Case studies
   - Grounded theory
   - Narrative analysis
3. Collect rich, detailed data
4. Look for:
   - Surprising patterns
   - Contradictions to theory
   - Emergent themes
   - Process mechanisms
   - Contextual variations
5. Develop preliminary concepts and relationships
6. Use findings to generate testable hypotheses

**Strengths:**
- Captures complexity and context
- Reveals unanticipated phenomena
- Gives voice to participants' perspectives
- Generates new concepts and theories

**Best Practices:**
- Maintain reflexivity about researcher assumptions
- Use multiple data sources (triangulation)
- Seek disconfirming cases
- Keep detailed field notes
- Iterate between data collection and analysis

## H9: Assemble Novel Datasets

**Core Principle:** Forge new datasets by linking disparate sources or mining raw data.

**Application:** New research opportunities emerge when researchers create datasets by linking previously separate sources, digitizing analog records, or aggregating scattered information. The dataset itself becomes an innovation.

**Key Questions:**
- What data sources exist but haven't been connected?
- What historical records could be digitized?
- What large-scale data could be assembled from distributed sources?
- What combinations of data would enable new questions?
- What data is collected but not analyzed?

**Examples:**
- **Genomics:** Assembling databases linking genetic variants to clinical outcomes, enabling genome-wide association studies
- **Digital Humanities:** Digitizing historical newspapers to enable large-scale analysis of cultural trends
- **Climate Science:** Integrating satellite data, weather stations, and historical records to analyze long-term patterns
- **Social Science:** Linking administrative records across agencies to study life course trajectories
- **LLM Research:** Nexus exemplifies this by linking previously disconnected data sources to create new hypothesis generation opportunities

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Identify valuable data that exists in silos
2. Assess feasibility of integration:
   - Technical compatibility
   - Legal/ethical permissions
   - Resource requirements
3. Develop integration methodology:
   - Common identifiers
   - Harmonization procedures
   - Quality control
4. Create documentation and metadata
5. Share dataset with community when appropriate
6. Design studies leveraging unique features

**Types of Novel Datasets:**
- **Linked Administrative Records:** Connecting government databases
- **Historical Digitization:** Converting analog archives to digital
- **Sensor Networks:** Aggregating distributed measurements
- **Crowdsourced Data:** Collecting contributions from many sources
- **Scraped Data:** Systematically gathering public information
- **Multi-Modal Integration:** Combining different data types (text, images, sensors)

**Considerations:**
- Privacy and ethical implications
- Data quality and bias
- Sustainability and maintenance
- Access and sharing policies

## H10: Explore Novel Techniques

**Core Principle:** Develop ideas enabled by technological advances.

**Application:** Technological advances create new research possibilities. New instruments enable better measurement, computational power enables complex analyses, and algorithmic innovations reveal patterns previously hidden.

**Key Questions:**
- What measurement technologies have recently emerged?
- What computational methods are now feasible?
- What analytical techniques could be applied to existing questions?
- How might new tools change what we can observe?
- What becomes possible with current technology that wasn't before?

**Examples:**
- **Neuroscience:** fMRI enabling real-time brain activity measurement during cognitive tasks
- **Genomics:** CRISPR enabling targeted gene editing for causal inference
- **Social Science:** Natural language processing enabling large-scale text analysis
- **Astronomy:** Advanced telescopes detecting exoplanets through new imaging techniques
- **Psychology:** Experience sampling via smartphones enabling real-time mood tracking

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Monitor technological developments in:
   - Measurement instruments
   - Computational capabilities
   - Analytical algorithms
   - Data collection platforms
2. Identify research questions that were previously infeasible
3. Assess:
   - What the new technique enables
   - What validation is needed
   - What limitations remain
4. Design studies that leverage the technical advance
5. Develop best practices for the new method

**Categories of Technical Advances:**
- **Measurement:** New sensors, imaging, recording devices
- **Computation:** Processing power, storage, cloud computing
- **Algorithms:** Machine learning, optimization, simulation
- **Platforms:** Online experiments, mobile apps, IoT devices
- **Analysis:** Statistical methods, visualization, modeling

**Integration with Other Heuristics:**
- Often reveals anomalies (H1) previously unmeasurable
- Enables assembling novel datasets (H9)
- Makes simulations feasible (H12)

## H11: Think Through Action

**Core Principle:** Build a prototype or run a pilot study for rapid insights.

**Application:** Rather than extensive planning before any empirical work, quickly create preliminary versions and learn from their performance. This iterative approach treats early research attempts as learning opportunities rather than definitive tests.

**Key Questions:**
- What minimal version could we test quickly?
- What would we learn from an initial attempt?
- How can we fail fast and learn cheaply?
- What assumptions can be tested with a pilot?
- What refinements does early feedback suggest?

**Examples:**
- **Design Research:** Creating low-fidelity prototypes to test concepts before full development
- **Intervention Research:** Running small pilot studies to refine protocols before large trials
- **Software Engineering:** Agile development with rapid iterations based on user feedback
- **Education:** Testing new curricula with single classes before district-wide implementation
- **LLM Research:** Liu et al. (2025) integrates preliminary validation on empirical datasets into ideation, showing pilot studies can guide toward more feasible ideas

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Identify core assumptions requiring testing
2. Design minimal viable test:
   - What's the simplest version?
   - What's the fastest timeline?
   - What's the smallest sample?
3. Execute quickly, prioritizing learning over perfection
4. Systematically document:
   - What worked
   - What didn't
   - Why
   - What to change
5. Refine based on lessons
6. Iterate: repeat the cycle
7. Scale up when confident in approach

**Types of Action-Based Learning:**
- **Prototyping:** Creating preliminary versions
- **Pilot Studies:** Small-scale trials
- **Field Tests:** Real-world implementation
- **Simulation:** Computational experiments (see H12)
- **Proof of Concept:** Demonstrating feasibility

**Benefits:**
- Rapid feedback
- Early failure detection
- Assumption testing
- Refinement before major investment
- Practical feasibility assessment

**Best Practices:**
- Set clear learning objectives
- Document everything
- Embrace failure as information
- Iterate systematically, not randomly
- Know when to pivot vs. persevere

## H12: Computer Simulation

**Core Principle:** Build a computational model to explore complex systems that are difficult to test in the real world.

**Application:** Computational models allow exploration of systems too complex, dangerous, expensive, or slow to study empirically. Simulations can reveal emergent properties, test parameter spaces, and generate predictions for empirical validation.

**Key Questions:**
- What processes can be modeled computationally?
- What parameters affect system behavior?
- What happens under extreme or impossible conditions?
- What emergent properties arise from component interactions?
- What predictions can guide empirical research?

**Examples:**
- **Epidemiology:** Modeling disease spread under different intervention scenarios before implementation
- **Climate Science:** Simulating climate responses to various emissions scenarios
- **Economics:** Agent-based models exploring market dynamics and policy impacts
- **Molecular Biology:** Simulating protein folding to understand structure-function relationships
- **Chemistry:** MOOSE-Chem uses simulated experimental feedback to refine and rank hypotheses, treating simulation as proxy for costly lab experiments

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Identify phenomenon suitable for simulation:
   - Too complex for analytical solutions
   - Too expensive/dangerous for experimentation
   - Requires exploration of parameter space
2. Develop computational model:
   - Define components and rules
   - Parameterize based on empirical data
   - Validate against known cases
3. Explore through simulation:
   - Vary parameters systematically
   - Test extreme conditions
   - Identify sensitivity
   - Discover emergent properties
4. Generate hypotheses from simulation results
5. Design empirical studies to test predictions

**Types of Simulation:**
- **Agent-Based:** Individual entities following rules
- **System Dynamics:** Stocks, flows, feedback loops
- **Monte Carlo:** Random sampling for probabilistic systems
- **Discrete Event:** Sequential processes and events
- **Mathematical Models:** Differential equations and analytical models

**Strengths:**
- Explore impossible scenarios
- Test many parameters quickly
- Isolate causal mechanisms
- Generate unexpected insights
- Cost-effective exploration

**Limitations:**
- Model validity depends on assumptions
- Emergent complexity may not match reality
- Still requires empirical validation
- Can mislead if assumptions wrong

**Integration with Other Heuristics:**
- Tests theories from conflict resolution (H4)
- Explores formal models (H18)
- Probes systems with controlled changes (H14)

## Using These Heuristics Together

These data-driven heuristics form a comprehensive approach to empirical discovery:

1. **H8** generates rich initial insights through qualitative exploration
2. **H9** creates datasets to test emerging ideas at scale
3. **H10** enables measurement and analysis previously impossible
4. **H11** tests ideas rapidly through action
5. **H12** explores systems computationally when direct study is infeasible

**Workflow Example:**
1. Begin with qualitative exploration (H8) to understand phenomenon
2. Identify need for quantitative data (H9) to test patterns
3. Use novel measurement techniques (H10) to collect data
4. Run pilots (H11) to refine measurement approaches
5. Build simulation (H12) to explore mechanisms and generate predictions
6. Return to empirical data to validate simulation predictions

This category emphasizes that data is not just evidence for testing hypotheses—it's a source of hypothesis generation itself. Active engagement with data, whether through collection, analysis, experimentation, or simulation, drives scientific discovery.
