# Reinterpreting Past Research (H4-H7)

The existing body of literature is a vast resource for generating new ideas. These heuristics are about mining past work for new inspiration.

**Usage Note**: Be selective—choose the most relevant heuristic and develop ideas thoroughly. Quality and depth are more important than using multiple heuristics or generating many ideas.

## H4: Generate Theories from Conflict

**Core Principle:** Integrate contradictory findings from different sources.

**Application:** When different studies reach opposite conclusions or conflicting results, the contradiction itself becomes a research opportunity. Understanding why results differ can reveal boundary conditions, moderating variables, or more nuanced theoretical frameworks.

**Key Questions:**
- Which studies in the field contradict each other?
- What methodological differences might explain the contradictions?
- Are there contextual factors that determine which finding holds?
- Could both findings be correct under different conditions?
- What higher-order theory could explain when each result occurs?

**Examples:**
- **Psychology:** Reconciling studies showing both positive and negative effects of multitasking by identifying task complexity as a moderator
  - *Hypothesis*: "Multitasking will improve performance on simple, well-practiced tasks but impair performance on complex, novel tasks"
- **Medicine:** Resolving conflicting efficacy results for a drug by discovering genetic variants that predict response
  - *Hypothesis*: "Drug X will show high efficacy in patients with genotype AA but low efficacy in patients with genotype BB"
- **Economics:** Explaining contradictory findings on minimum wage effects by considering regional labor market conditions
  - *Hypothesis*: "Minimum wage increases will reduce employment in regions with tight labor markets but have minimal employment effects in regions with slack labor markets"
- **Machine Learning:** Addressing conflicting results on neural network generalization by examining dataset characteristics and architecture choices
  - *Hypothesis*: "Deep networks will outperform shallow networks on high-dimensional data with hierarchical structure but show no advantage on tabular data with flat feature relationships"

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Conduct systematic literature review to identify contradictions
2. Create a matrix comparing studies: methods, samples, contexts, findings
3. Identify potential explanatory variables (moderators, mediators)
4. Develop integrative theory that specifies boundary conditions
5. Design studies to test the integrative framework

**Common Sources of Contradiction:**
- Different operationalizations of constructs
- Varying sample characteristics
- Contextual or temporal differences
- Methodological strengths and limitations
- Publication bias toward novel findings

## H5: Create Novel Syntheses

**Core Principle:** Connect concepts from unrelated research to find new insights.

**Application:** By connecting ideas across disciplinary boundaries or combining concepts that haven't been studied together, researchers can generate entirely new research questions and theoretical perspectives.

**Key Questions:**
- What concepts from different fields share underlying similarities?
- How might a theory from Field A apply to problems in Field B?
- What happens when we combine methods from different domains?
- Can tools or frameworks from one area solve problems in another?
- What analogies exist between seemingly unrelated phenomena?

**Examples:**
- **Interdisciplinary Synthesis:** Applying network theory from physics to understand social networks, creating social network analysis
- **Cross-Domain Application:** Using evolutionary biology principles to understand cultural change
- **Methodological Integration:** Combining ethnographic methods with computational text analysis for digital anthropology
- **Conceptual Bridging:** Linking behavioral economics and public health to improve medical decision-making
- **LLM-Based Ideation:** Scideator recombines research facets (purpose of one study with mechanism of another); MOOSE-Chem combines background and inspiration knowledge from different papers

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Read broadly across disciplines
2. Identify conceptual similarities or analogies
3. Translate concepts into new domain language
4. Test whether the transferred concept provides explanatory power
5. Refine the synthesis based on domain-specific constraints

**Types of Synthesis:**
- **Conceptual:** Combining theoretical frameworks
- **Methodological:** Integrating different research approaches
- **Empirical:** Linking different types of data or evidence
- **Analogical:** Applying patterns from one domain to another

**Warning:** Ensure syntheses are substantive, not superficial. The combination should generate genuine new insight, not just terminological overlap.

## H6: Interpret Incidental Findings

**Core Principle:** Examine unexpected interactions or side effects in prior studies.

**Application:** Research often uncovers findings beyond the primary hypothesis. These incidental discoveries—mentioned in footnotes, limitations sections, or supplementary materials—can become the foundation for new research directions.

**Key Questions:**
- What unexpected patterns appeared in the data but weren't the focus?
- What did researchers mention in passing or in limitations sections?
- What interactions or side effects were noted but not explored?
- What secondary outcomes showed interesting patterns?
- What null results might actually be meaningful?

**Examples:**
- **Pharmacology:** Discovering Viagra's primary use from side effects noted in cardiac trials
- **Psychology:** Noticing that control group participants showed unexpected patterns, leading to new theories about placebo effects
- **Machine Learning:** Observing that models trained on one task unexpectedly perform well on unrelated tasks, leading to transfer learning research
- **Ecology:** Finding unexpected species interactions while studying primary predator-prey relationships

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Read papers for more than just main findings
2. Pay special attention to:
   - Methods sections (what was controlled or held constant)
   - Results sections (all tables, not just highlighted findings)
   - Discussion limitations (what couldn't be explained)
   - Supplementary materials (additional analyses)
3. Ask what these incidental findings might mean
4. Design focused studies to investigate them directly

**Where to Look:**
- Null results (often meaningful)
- Unexpected interactions
- Secondary dependent variables
- Control condition effects
- Participant feedback or observations
- Failed manipulations (why did they fail?)

## H7: Reorganize Current Knowledge

**Core Principle:** Reorganize existing literature of one field and chart new research directions.

**Application:** By creating new organizational schemes—taxonomies, frameworks, causal chains, or concept maps—researchers can reveal gaps, patterns, and opportunities that linear reading of papers would miss.

**Key Questions:**
- How can this literature be organized differently?
- What patterns emerge from new classification schemes?
- What gaps appear when knowledge is restructured?
- What connections become visible with different organization?
- What questions has the field not asked?

**Examples:**
- **Meta-Analysis:** Organizing effect sizes across studies to identify moderators and reveal patterns
- **Systematic Review:** Creating taxonomies of approaches to reveal what hasn't been tried
- **Concept Mapping:** Visualizing relationships between constructs to identify missing links
- **Historical Analysis:** Organizing research chronologically to understand evolution of ideas and identify abandoned directions worth revisiting
- **LLM-Based Approaches:** Chain-of-Ideas structures literature into sequential chains; CoQuest organizes literature into graphs to uncover gaps

**Implementation Strategy:**
1. Collect comprehensive literature in your domain
2. Choose alternative organization scheme:
   - By methodology rather than topic
   - By theoretical assumption rather than finding
   - By historical progression
   - By level of analysis
   - By outcome patterns
3. Create visual representation (matrix, network, timeline)
4. Identify:
   - Cells or nodes with no research
   - Connections that haven't been explored
   - Patterns across the organization
   - Assumptions shared by entire sections

**Types of Reorganization:**
- **Conceptual:** By theoretical frameworks or constructs
- **Methodological:** By research designs or measurement approaches
- **Temporal:** By chronological development
- **Structural:** By causal relationships or mechanisms
- **Hierarchical:** By levels of analysis or abstraction

**Tools:**
- Concept matrices
- Literature networks
- Citation analysis
- Topic modeling
- Systematic coding schemes

## Using These Heuristics Together

These literature-based heuristics complement each other:

1. **H4** identifies contradictions to resolve
2. **H5** brings in external ideas to create resolution
3. **H6** finds overlooked details that might explain contradictions
4. **H7** reveals larger patterns and gaps across literature

**Workflow Example:**
1. Systematically organize literature (H7)
2. Identify contradictory findings in the organized framework (H4)
3. Look for incidental findings that might explain contradictions (H6)
4. Import concepts from other fields to create integrative theory (H5)

This category transforms passive literature review into active knowledge generation, ensuring that existing research catalyzes rather than constrains new thinking.
